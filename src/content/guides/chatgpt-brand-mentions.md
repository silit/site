A brand mention in ChatGPT is your name inside an answer. A customer asks which accountant, hotel or invoicing app to choose, ChatGPT writes a few lines, and your business is in them or it is not.

Nothing you already use records that moment. There is no report of it in Search Console and no line for it in your analytics. This guide explains what a mention is and is not, how to find yours, what to write down so you can track them, and what makes ChatGPT name a business more often.

## Mention, citation, click: three different things

They are often mixed up, and they are won in different ways.

| | What it is | Who can see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mention | Your name in the text of the answer | Only someone who reads the answer |
| Citation | Your page listed among the sources the answer was built from | Only someone who reads the answer |
| Click | A person follows a link from the answer to your site | Your analytics, usually as a visit from chatgpt.com |

You can be mentioned without being cited: ChatGPT read about you on a review site and named you, and the source it lists is the review site. You can also be cited without being mentioned in a way that helps, for example as the source for a fact in an answer that recommends someone else.

Clicks are the only one of the three your tools can count, and they undercount the rest. Many people who read an answer do not click anything. They remember a name, and later they search for it or type the address. That visit shows up as a branded search or direct traffic, with nothing to tell you it started in a chat window.

So the number worth tracking is not traffic from ChatGPT. It is how often you are named.

## How to find your mentions

There are three ways, from free and slow to paid and automatic.

**Ask by hand.** Open a new chat with search on, logged out or in a temporary chat so your own history does not steer it. Ask what a customer would ask, without your brand name in the question. Read the answer, then ask again in a fresh chat, because the second answer is often different. One warning for all three: do not ask ChatGPT "do you know my company?" and count the reply. Asked by name, it will find something to say about almost any business with a website. A mention only counts when the customer did not name you first.

## What to write down

A mention on its own tells you little. Five things recorded the same way each time turn it into something you can compare month to month.

| What to record | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The question, word for word | Small changes in wording change the answer |
| Named or not | The headline number: answers that name you out of answers collected |
| Your place in the list | First of three is not the same as last of six |
| Who else was named | These are your real rivals for this question, and they are often not the ones you expect |
| The sources it listed | They show where ChatGPT learned what it said |

Ask each question at least twice. If you are named in one answer out of two, write down one out of two. That is a more honest number than "yes".

Keep the list of questions short and fixed. Five questions that customers really ask, checked once a month, tell you more than fifty checked once.

## What gets a business mentioned more

With search on, ChatGPT does not answer from memory alone. It runs web searches, opens a handful of pages and writes its answer from what it finds there. That is why the sources column matters more than anything else you record: it is the list of pages that decided the answer.

**Get onto the pages it reads.** If the same round-up article, directory or review site appears in the sources every time, and your rivals are on it and you are not, that is the gap. Ask to be added, claim your listing or collect reviews there. This is the fastest way to earn mentions, and it happens on someone else's website.

**Make your own pages readable.** ChatGPT search reaches sites through a crawler called OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI [states](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) that sites opted out of it are not shown in ChatGPT search answers, so check that your robots.txt does not shut it out. Check also that your words are in the page code and do not appear only after scripts have run, because a crawler that does not run scripts reads an empty page.

**Say who you are in one plain paragraph.** What you do, for whom and where, near the top of your home page, in text. Use the same wording on your listings. When every source describes you the same way, ChatGPT repeats it. When they differ, it guesses.

**Answer the question on a page of your own.** If customers ask "how much does it cost", a page that answers in its first two sentences can be cited as the source. That earns the citation, and a cited business is usually a mentioned one. Our guide on [how to get cited by ChatGPT](/guides/how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt/) covers how it picks those pages.

Third-party pages mostly earn you mentions. Your own pages mostly earn you citations. You need both, and most businesses that are missing from answers are missing from the third-party pages first.

## What does not move mentions

- **An llms.txt file.** It is harmless and quick to add, but so far no major AI company has said its answers rely on it, and Google has said the file is not needed to appear in its AI features.
- **Telling ChatGPT about your business in a chat.** What it remembers from a conversation belongs to that one user. It does not reach anyone else's answers.
- **Blocking or unblocking GPTBot.** GPTBot collects pages for training. OpenAI treats it separately from OAI-SearchBot, and each setting is independent of the other.
- **Repeating your brand name on your own pages.** ChatGPT names businesses it meets on the pages it reads for a question. Saying your name more often on a page it does not read changes nothing.

## How often to check

Once a month is enough for most businesses, on the same questions each time. Check again a few weeks after you change something that should matter, such as a new listing or a rewritten page. The pages ChatGPT reads have to be crawled again before an answer can change, so a check the next morning tells you nothing.

One answer is a snapshot. The direction over three or four checks is what tells you whether the work is paying off.
