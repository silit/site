Most hair transplant patients go home within a day or two of surgery, often to another country. From then on, the aftercare sheet is the clinic's voice at two in the morning. Clear written instructions reduce worried messages, protect the grafts and make the twelve-month result more likely to match the plan.

This guide covers what a hair transplant aftercare document should include and how to structure it. Timings below are typical examples; your surgeon's own protocol decides the details.

## Structure it as a timeline

Patients do not read aftercare once. They come back to it on day 3, day 10 and month 4 with a specific question. A timeline lets them find their day quickly.

| When | What patients need to know |
| --- | --- |
| First nights | Sleep with the head raised, often around 45 degrees with a travel pillow. Do not touch or scratch the grafts. Use any spray exactly as instructed |
| First days | Some forehead swelling is common and usually settles within a few days. Take medicines as prescribed |
| First wash | Usually done at the clinic or on the day the clinic specifies, then repeated at home as instructed |
| First two weeks | Gentle daily washing with no rubbing; pat dry. Crusts usually loosen and fall away during the second week |
| First month | Avoid swimming, saunas, direct sun and heavy exercise until the clinic says otherwise |
| Months 1 to 3 | Shedding of transplanted hairs is common and expected. The roots stay in place |
| Months 3 to 4 | New growth usually starts, often thin at first |
| Months 6 to 12 | Visible change and thickening. The crown can take longer than the front |
| Around 12 months and beyond | The final result is usually judged at a year or later |

## The washing routine

Washing is where most questions come from, so give it its own section with numbered steps. Say which product to use, how to apply it (for example, foam or lotion patted on, not rubbed), how long to leave it, how to rinse (gentle water from a cup or low-pressure shower) and how to dry. Photos or simple drawings help more than extra words.

## What to avoid

Keep this as a short list patients can scan:

- Touching, picking or scratching the recipient area
- Tight hats or helmets until the clinic allows them
- Alcohol and smoking in the period your surgeon advises
- Heavy exercise, bending and lifting in the first days
- Swimming pools, sea, saunas and direct sun in the first weeks

## When to contact the clinic

This section should stand out on the page. Typical reasons to call right away:

- Bleeding that does not stop with gentle pressure
- Increasing redness, warmth, pus or a fever, which can be signs of infection
- Pain that gets worse instead of better
- Swelling around the eyes that keeps increasing

Give a direct phone or WhatsApp number and say who answers it.

## Set expectations about growth

Shedding in the first months worries patients more than anything else. A simple growth timeline that says shedding is expected, and when new hair usually appears, prevents many anxious messages. Ask patients for photos at fixed points, such as 3, 6, 9 and 12 months, so progress can be compared.

## Make it easy to carry

International patients will read aftercare on their phone, not on paper in a drawer. Send the instructions as phone-sized screens on WhatsApp as well as a printed sheet. Use the same terms as in the consent form and the treatment plan, and provide them in the patient's language.
