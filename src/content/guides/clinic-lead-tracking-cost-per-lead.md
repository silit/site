Most clinics know two numbers well: what they spend on ads each month, and how many patients sat in the chair. What happens in between is often spread across WhatsApp chats, form emails and Instagram messages on several phones. Without that middle part, you cannot tell which ads bring patients and which ones only bring messages.

This guide covers what to record for every enquiry, how to work out cost per lead and cost per booking, and the mistakes we see most often in clinics that treat patients from abroad.

## Record every enquiry in one place

A lead is anyone who contacts you about treatment: a lead form, a WhatsApp message, an Instagram DM, a phone call. Give each one a row on the day it arrives, in one shared sheet or system, whichever channel it came through and whichever coordinator picked it up.

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Date received | Every report starts here: leads per month, reply speed and time to booking |
| Source | Meta lead ads, Google Ads, Instagram DM, website form, WhatsApp, referral. Without it there is no cost per lead |
| Campaign or ad | Shows which campaign works inside a channel, not just which channel |
| Treatment of interest | An implant enquiry and a whitening enquiry are worth very different amounts |
| Coordinator | Shows who owns each lead, and whose follow-ups are slipping |
| First reply | How fast someone answered: under 5 minutes, under an hour, same day, next day or later |
| Status | Where the lead stands now (see below) |
| Plan value | The price on the treatment plan you sent |
| Booking date and revenue | Turns a list of conversations into a sales report |
| Lost reason | Price, another clinic, no reply, not ready yet, medical reason, travel or dates |
| Next follow-up | The date someone will contact the lead again |

### Keep the status list short and fixed

Pick statuses from a dropdown rather than typing them, or "plan sent", "sent plan" and "PLAN SENT" become three different statuses. A list that fits most clinics:

New, Contacted, Photos received, Plan sent, Follow-up, Booked, Treated, Lost, Not qualified.

"Not qualified" matters more than it looks. People asking about a treatment you do not offer, or messages that were never real enquiries, should not count against a campaign in the same way as patients who chose another clinic.

## How to work out cost per lead

Cost per lead is the ad spend for a channel or campaign in a period, divided by the leads it brought in over the same period.

**Cost per lead = ad spend ÷ leads**

Say you spent £1,200 on Meta lead ads in March and logged 40 leads with Meta as the source. Your cost per lead for March is £30.

Do the same for every channel and campaign. This is why the source and campaign fields matter so much: if half your team writes "Instagram" and the other half writes "Meta" for the same ads, the spend and the leads will never meet.

## Cost per booking matters more

A cheap lead that never replies costs more than an expensive one that books. The number to steer by is cost per booking.

**Cost per booking = ad spend ÷ bookings from those leads**

If that £1,200 of Meta spend brought 4 bookings, each booking cost £300. Now imagine a Google campaign at £45 per lead where one lead in five books. Its cost per booking is £225, so it is the better campaign despite the higher cost per lead.

Then look at what the bookings were worth.

**Return on ad spend = revenue from those leads ÷ ad spend**

Four bookings worth £14,000 against £1,200 of spend is a return of about 11.7 times.

## Count bookings against the month the lead arrived

Patients who travel for treatment rarely book in the week they enquire. A lead from January may pay a deposit in March and fly out in May. If you count March bookings against March spend, the numbers jump around and credit the wrong month.

Count bookings and revenue against the month the lead first arrived instead. January's figures keep improving for a few months as January leads book, and that is the honest picture: it shows what each month of advertising produced. Just remember that the most recent months always look worse than they will end up.

## Measure reply speed

The first reply is the one step you control completely. Record it in a few simple bands rather than exact minutes, and compare it by coordinator and by source. If leads from one channel arrive in the evening and wait until the next afternoon, the channel may not be the problem.

## Give every open lead a next date

Many leads are not lost to another clinic. They go quiet after "let me think about it" and nobody writes back. The fix is simple: every open lead gets a next follow-up date, and each morning someone works through the leads that are due today or overdue, oldest first.

## Record why leads are lost

When a lead says no, choose a reason from a short list. After a few months a pattern usually shows. Lots of price losses can mean the plan did not explain what the price includes. Lots of no replies often point back to slow first replies. Leads lost to travel or dates may come back next season, so keep them.

## Common mistakes

- Logging only the leads that look promising, which makes cost per lead look better than it is
- Free text for source and status, so nothing adds up at the end of the month
- Judging channels on cost per lead alone
- Counting bookings in the month they were paid instead of the month the lead arrived
- No owner for each lead, so follow-ups fall between coordinators
- Patient details on personal phones instead of one shared place the clinic controls

## Spreadsheet or CRM?

A CRM earns its cost once several coordinators handle hundreds of leads a month and you want leads to arrive and reminders to go out automatically. Before that point, a well-built spreadsheet does the job, as long as everyone uses the same lists and fills it in every day. It is also a cheap way to learn which fields you really need before paying for a system.

Whichever you use, patient details are personal health data. Keep them in an account your clinic controls, limit who can open the file, and follow your local data protection rules, such as the UK GDPR, the EU GDPR or KVKK in Turkey.
